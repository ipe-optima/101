### A-1 | Model Set A | 5
*Passage: "The Story of Paper" (paragraphs A–D). Written for this mock; questions A-1 to A-6 refer to it.*

**A** Before paper, people wrote on whatever material was at hand: clay tablets in Mesopotamia, strips of bamboo and pieces of silk in China, and papyrus in Egypt. Each had a drawback. Clay was heavy, bamboo was bulky and silk was expensive, while papyrus, made from a reed that grew mainly along the Nile, was brittle and difficult to produce elsewhere.

**B** Chinese records say that in 105 AD a court official named Cai Lun presented a new writing material to the Han emperor. He made it by pounding tree bark, old rags and worn-out fishing nets into a pulp, spreading the pulp on a screen and leaving it to dry. Older paper fragments have since been unearthed, so Cai Lun probably improved an existing process rather than invented one. Even so, his method used such cheap ingredients that paper became affordable for ordinary people.

**C** For several centuries the craft stayed within East Asia. It reached the Islamic world in the eighth century, probably through captured craftsmen after the Battle of Talas in 751 AD. Mills soon operated in Samarkand and Baghdad, and the skill then travelled across North Africa to Spain. The first paper mills in Italy appeared in the thirteenth century.

**D** Demand soared in Europe after the printing press arrived around 1450, and rags, the usual raw material, became scarce. Inventors therefore looked for alternatives, and by the middle of the nineteenth century wood pulp had begun to replace rags. Machines that produced a continuous roll of paper had already replaced the slow work of making one sheet at a time. Today paper is one of the most widely recycled materials, since used sheets can be pulped again and turned into new ones.

Write TRUE, FALSE or NOT GIVEN.

- (i) Papyrus could be produced easily in any part of the world.
- (ii) Cai Lun made paper partly from old fishing nets.
- (iii) Cai Lun's paper is the oldest paper ever found.
- (iv) Chinese rulers punished anyone who revealed the secret of papermaking.
- (v) Rags became scarce in Europe because demand for paper rose sharply after printing began.

#### Answer
| Statement | Answer |
|---|---|
| (i) | FALSE |
| (ii) | TRUE |
| (iii) | FALSE |
| (iv) | NOT GIVEN |
| (v) | TRUE |

#### Explanation
- **(i) False:** paragraph A says papyrus grew mainly along the Nile and was difficult to produce elsewhere.
- **(ii) True:** paragraph B lists tree bark, old rags and worn-out fishing nets.
- **(iii) False:** older paper fragments have since been unearthed, so his was not the oldest.
- **(iv) Not Given:** the passage says the craft stayed in East Asia but never gives a reason or mentions any punishment.
- **(v) True:** paragraph D links the soaring demand after the printing press to the shortage of rags.

## Error Correction

### A-7 | Model Set A | 5
Each sentence contains one error. Identify it and rewrite the sentence correctly.

- (a) The manager, along with his assistants, are preparing the annual report.
- (b) She suggested me to take the evening train.
- (c) Neither the teacher nor the students was aware of the schedule change.
- (d) I look forward to meet you at the conference.
- (e) He is more taller than his elder brother.

#### Answer
| | Corrected sentence | Error |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | The manager, along with his assistants, **is** preparing the annual report. | the subject is the singular *manager*; *along with* does not make it plural |
| (b) | She suggested **that I take** the evening train. | *suggest* takes a *that*-clause with the base verb, not object + to-infinitive |
| (c) | Neither the teacher nor the students **were** aware of the schedule change. | the verb agrees with the nearer subject, *students* |
| (d) | I look forward to **meeting** you at the conference. | *to* is a preposition here, so a gerund follows |
| (e) | He is **taller** than his elder brother. | double comparative: use *-er* or *more*, never both |

#### Explanation
- Name the faulty rule when you answer; it often earns the mark.
- **(b):** "that I should take" is also acceptable.
- **(c):** with *neither … nor*, the verb agrees with the subject closer to it.

## Grammar Usage
desc: Tenses, articles, subject–verb agreement, causatives, subjunctives and modals.

### A-9 | Model Set A | 3
Rewrite the sentences correctly.

- (a) If I will see him tomorrow, I will give him your message.
- (b) She has been living in Sylhet since five years.
- (c) By the time we reached the station, the train already left.

#### Answer
- **(a)** If I **see** him tomorrow, I will give him your message.
- **(b)** She has been living in Sylhet **for** five years.
- **(c)** By the time we reached the station, the train **had** already left.

#### Explanation
- **(a):** the first conditional has the pattern below. *Will* is not used in the *if*-clause.

:::eq
If + present simple, will + base verb
:::

- **(b):** *for* goes with a period of time (five years); *since* goes with a starting point (since 2021).
- **(c):** the past perfect (*had left*) shows the action that happened earlier of two past actions.

## Sentence Transformation
desc: Converting between simple, compound and complex sentences.

### A-13 | Model Set A | 2
Convert the sentences according to the instructions. (a) Because the road was flooded, the buses were cancelled. (Simple) (b) He worked hard, so he passed the exam. (Complex)

#### Answer
- **(a)** **Because of the flooded road**, the buses were cancelled.
- **(b)** **Because he worked hard**, he passed the exam.

#### Explanation
- A **simple** sentence has one clause, so the *because* clause is reduced to *because of* + noun phrase.
- A **complex** sentence has a main clause and a dependent clause; *so* (a coordinator) becomes *because* or *since* (a subordinator).

### A-14 | Model Set A | 2
Convert the sentences according to the instructions. (a) Walking to the station, I met an old friend. (Complex) (b) If you do not wake up early, you will miss the bus. (Compound)

#### Answer
- **(a)** **While I was walking to the station**, I met an old friend.
- **(b)** **Wake up early, or** you will miss the bus.

#### Explanation
- **(a):** the participle phrase becomes a full subordinate clause with its own subject and verb.
- **(b):** a **compound** sentence joins two main clauses with a coordinator; an *if not* condition becomes an imperative + *or*.

### A-17 | Model Set A | 10
Write an argumentative essay of around 210 words: Mobile phones should be allowed in university classrooms. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

#### Answer
Mobile phones have become part of everyday life, yet most university teachers still ask students to switch them off in lectures. I agree to a large extent that phones should be allowed in classrooms, provided that clear rules govern their use.

The strongest argument is educational. A phone gives instant access to dictionaries, e-books and research articles, so a student can check an unfamiliar term without interrupting the teacher. Many lecturers also use quiz apps and shared documents, which make lessons more interactive. Moreover, students can photograph the board, record explanations with permission and store their notes safely.

On the other hand, phones can be a serious distraction. Messages, social media and games may draw attention away from the lesson, and a ringing phone disturbs classmates. Some students may even use phones to cheat during tests.

These risks, however, can be managed without a total ban. Teachers can allow phones only for learning tasks, insist on silent mode and collect devices before examinations. Students who misuse them can be warned or penalised, which also teaches responsibility.

In conclusion, a blanket ban ignores the educational value of technology. Universities should permit phones in classrooms under sensible and firmly enforced rules, so that students gain the benefits without losing their focus.

#### Explanation
- "To what extent" invites a clear position (here, agreement to a large extent) that stays consistent.
- Structure: thesis, strongest argument, counter-argument, how to manage the risk, conclusion.
- The model essay is about 209 words.
- Marking guide: content and arguments 4, organisation 3, language and vocabulary 3.

### A-17-OR | Model Set A | 10 | or
Write a compare–contrast essay of around 210 words on "Public Transport vs Private Cars for Daily Commuters in Dhaka".

#### Answer
Every morning, millions of commuters in Dhaka must choose between public transport and their own cars. Both options serve the same purpose, getting people to work or study, but they differ in cost, comfort and impact on the city.

The two share one problem: heavy congestion. Whichever is chosen, a journey that should take half an hour can easily take two.

The differences, however, are considerable. A private car offers privacy, comfort and flexible timing, since the owner can leave whenever he wishes. Public transport, in contrast, follows fixed routes and schedules, and buses are often overcrowded. Cost is another difference: a car demands fuel, parking and maintenance, whereas a bus fare is far cheaper. Furthermore, a single bus carries dozens of passengers on the road space that holds only a few cars, so private cars worsen congestion and pollution while public transport reduces them. Finally, drivers avoid the crowds but spend hours behind the wheel, whereas bus passengers can read or rest if they find a seat.

In conclusion, private cars give comfort and convenience, but public transport is cheaper and better for the city. If buses and metro services become reliable, clean and well managed, more commuters will gladly leave their cars at home.

#### Explanation
- Point-by-point structure: introduction, similarity, differences (comfort, cost, effect on the city), conclusion.
- Contrast words used: *however*, *in contrast*, *whereas*; similarity words: *both*, *the two*.
- The model essay is about 206 words.
- Choose either A-17 or A-17-OR in the exam, not both.

## Grammar Usage

### B-9 | Model Set B | 3

### B-13 | Model Set B | 2
Convert the sentences according to the instructions. (a) Despite being tired, she finished the assignment. (Complex) (b) The weather was bad, but the match continued. (Simple)

#### Answer
- **(a)** **Although she was tired**, she finished the assignment.
- **(b)** **Despite the bad weather**, the match continued.

#### Explanation
- **(a):** *despite* + gerund becomes a concession clause with *although* and a full subject and verb.
- **(b):** the first clause of the compound sentence is reduced to a phrase with *despite* + noun phrase to make a simple sentence.

### B-14 | Model Set B | 2
Convert the sentences according to the instructions. (a) She was so shy that she could not speak in public. (Simple) (b) Work steadily, and you will finish on time. (Complex)

#### Answer
- **(a)** She was **too shy to speak** in public.
- **(b)** **If you work steadily**, you will finish on time.

#### Explanation
- **(a):** *so … that* (complex) becomes *too … to* + base verb (simple).
- **(b):** an imperative + *and* expresses a condition, so it can be rewritten as an *if*-clause.

### B-17 | Model Set B | 10
Write a persuasive essay of around 230 words urging every university student to learn basic first aid.

#### Answer
Accidents do not announce themselves. A classmate may collapse in a crowded corridor, a cyclist may be knocked down outside the gate, or a relative may choke at the dinner table. In such moments the first few minutes decide whether a person recovers. For this reason, every university student should learn basic first aid.

First, first aid saves lives. Knowing how to stop bleeding, treat burns, perform CPR and recognise a stroke can keep a victim alive until help arrives, which in a congested city such as Dhaka may take a long time. Ordinary students are often the first responders because they happen to be present.

Second, the training is short, cheap and easy. A two-day workshop run by the Red Crescent or a medical college can teach the essentials, and its cost is tiny compared with the value of a single saved life. Moreover, these skills stay with a person for life, whatever career he or she chooses.

Third, first aid builds confidence and responsibility. A trained student does not freeze; he or she acts calmly and helps others to stay calm.

Some may say the syllabus is already crowded, but a few hours of training is a small price for the ability to save a life.

Therefore, universities should include first-aid training in their orientation programmes, and every student should enrol willingly. Let us learn today, because tomorrow someone may need our help.

#### Explanation
- A persuasive essay takes a firm position and urges action: hook and thesis, three reasons with evidence, an answer to a counter-argument, and a call to action.
- Persuasive language used: *should*, *therefore*, *let us*.
- The model essay is about 235 words.
- Marking guide: content and persuasion 4, organisation 3, language and vocabulary 3.

### B-17-OR | Model Set B | 10 | or
Write a paragraph of around 150 words on "Printed Books vs E-books".

#### Answer
Printed books and e-books are two popular ways of reading, and each has its own strengths. A printed book offers a pleasant, physical experience: readers can turn pages, underline passages and read for hours without eye strain or a low-battery warning. It also needs no device and makes a lovely gift. E-books, by contrast, are extremely convenient. A single tablet or phone can hold hundreds of titles, so a student can carry a whole library in a bag, search for a word in seconds and adjust the font size for comfortable reading. They are usually cheaper and can be downloaded instantly, even at midnight. However, screens can tire the eyes and tempt readers to open other apps, while printed books are heavier and take up shelf space. In my opinion, the choice depends on purpose: printed books suit deep, relaxed reading, whereas e-books suit travel and quick reference. Ideally, a reader should enjoy both.

#### Explanation
- A paragraph has one main idea: a topic sentence, supporting sentences (strengths of each), and a concluding sentence.
- Linking words used: *by contrast*, *however*, *whereas*.
- The model paragraph is about 154 words.
- Choose either B-17 or B-17-OR in the exam, not both.
